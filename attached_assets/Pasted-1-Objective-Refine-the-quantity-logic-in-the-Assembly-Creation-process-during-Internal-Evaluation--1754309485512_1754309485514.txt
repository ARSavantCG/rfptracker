1. Objective
Refine the quantity logic in the Assembly Creation process during Internal Evaluation to prioritize user-selected quantity units, preventing incorrect aggregation of mixed units (e.g., LF, SF).
________________________________________
2. Feature – Primary Quantity Source in Assemblies
Current Issue:
•	The system is auto-aggregating quantities (e.g., adding LF and SF together) when assembling components.
•	This auto-sum logic disrupts assemblies where items have different measurement units, resulting in misleading totals.
Desired Behavior:
•	When a user adds multiple items to an assembly, the quantity and unit of the first-clicked item will determine the assembly’s base quantity and unit.
•	Subsequent items added to the assembly will:
o	Adopt the assembly’s primary unit context for pricing calculations.
o	Calculate unit price contributions proportionally to the assembly's base quantity.
•	The total price of the assembly remains accurate by distributing additional item costs over the assembly's primary quantity.
________________________________________
3. Rules & Requirements:
•	First Click Rule:
o	When creating an assembly, the first item clicked defines:
	Quantity
	Unit of Measure (UOM)
o	This becomes the Assembly’s Base Quantity Context.
•	Subsequent Items:
o	Do not sum their quantities into the assembly total.
o	Instead, calculate their price contribution per the base quantity.
	Example:
	Assembly base: 100 LF (from first item clicked)
	Second item: 50 SF → Only its unit cost gets proportionally factored into the Assembly’s per LF pricing.
•	Unit Price Recalculation:
o	After adding all items, recalculate the assembly's total unit price based on the aggregate of all included item costs over the Assembly Base Quantity.
•	Unit Conversion Logic: (Optional Future Scope)
o	For advanced workflows, consider logic for unit conversions (e.g., LF to SF ratios), but exclude this in current implementation.
________________________________________
4. Developer Notes:
•	This logic should override any existing quantity-sum behaviors when assemblies have mixed units.
•	Maintain visual cues in UI to indicate:
o	Which item is defining the assembly’s unit and quantity.
o	How other items are being costed within that context.
•	Ensure this change does not disrupt assemblies where all items share the same UOM (fallback to current behavior).
